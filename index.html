Automated Investment Portfolio

A five-year backtest in Excel. I split $500,000 across 12 stocks in IT and healthcare, bought on Jan 21, 2020, sold half on Jan 24, 2025, and valued the other half at live prices. Every output is a formula, so changing a weight, price, or tax rate updates the whole workbook.

What the workbook covers

  • Portfolio Weights, shares bought, tiered transaction fee, 15% tax on the realized half, net gain, and % gain before and after tax, with an allocation check and chart.
  • LivePrices Current quotes that feed the Portfolio sheet through VLOOKUP.
  • Industry and Sector summaries Amount invested, gain, and average % gain, built with SUMIFS and AVERAGEIFS.
  • Rebalance Simulation Ranks every stock by return at Jan 24, 2023, sells the bottom 6, splits the proceeds equally across the top 6, and compares the result with buy-and-hold.
  • Excel
  • Diversification rules
  • Rebalancing
  • SUMIFS / VLOOKUP

Research Report: Data Ownership

A co-authored paper arguing that users should have legal ownership of the data they generate online, even if that changes how “free” internet services work. It combines a literature review with a direct argument built on property law and political theory.

The paper takes on the strongest objection head-on: that ownership would break the ad-funded model. It responds that these services are not free, since users already pay with their data, and that consent-based exchange would replace overcollection.

May 2025University projectPython

Customer Segmentation Using K-Means Clustering on Credit Card Data

A K-Means model that groups about 10,000 bank credit card customers by behavior, so offers can be tailored to each group and churn reduced. I removed ID and pre-computed classifier columns, encoded the categorical fields, standardized all 19 features, and used the elbow method to settle on five clusters.

Each cluster is profiled on credit limit, transaction amount and count, utilization, and retention, then paired with a recommendation. The standout is cluster 1: basic cards with only 67% of customers retained, the weakest of the five.

for col in df_cleaned.select_dtypes(include=["object"]).columns:
    df_cleaned[col] = LabelEncoder().fit_transform(df_cleaned[col])

features = df_cleaned.drop(columns=["Attrition_Flag"])
features_scaled = StandardScaler().fit_transform(features)

# Elbow method, K = 1 to 10
inertia = {k: KMeans(n_clusters=k, random_state=42, n_init=10).fit(features_scaled).inertia_

# Final model, K = 5

df_cleaned["Cluster"] = kmeans.fit_predict(features_scaled)
profiles = df_cleaned.groupby("Cluster").mean().round(2)
